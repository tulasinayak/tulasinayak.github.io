A little bit about me

I'm an AI Engineer based in Saarbrücken, Germany, finishing my M.Sc. in Computer Science at Saarland University. I've always been drawn to the point where research turns into something people can actually use, which is why I like taking LLM systems from an idea to a working product: multi-agent applications, retrieval-augmented pipelines, safety guardrails, and fine-tuned open-weight models such as Qwen and Llama.

My thesis at DFKI applies fine-tuned LLMs to real clinical interviews, and at CISPA I work with researchers from LMU Munich on LLM-driven feature discovery for drug research. Both taught me that a model is only as good as its evaluation: strong baselines, careful splits, and results you can trust.

When I'm not training models, I like to switch off properly. You'll usually find me on a hiking trail, painting, or exploring a new city.

I'm looking for a team where I can ship LLM and agent features that real users rely on.

Research projectLLMs as molecular feature generators for antimicrobial-activity prediction

  • Prompted six GPT-4.x and GPT-5.x models zero-shot through the OpenAI API to generate molecular descriptors from SMILES strings alone, then audited them against expert-selected RDKit descriptors and pretrained MolE embeddings.
  • Built the XGBoost pipeline that fuses LLM descriptors, MolE embeddings and bacterial-strain features on about 47,900 compound-strain pairs (1,197 compounds × 40 gut bacterial strains).
  • Designed the evaluation: scaffold-held-out splits so the model is tested on unseen chemical scaffolds, identical hyperparameter search for every configuration, and PR-AUC as the primary metric for imbalanced labels.
  • Showed that LLM-proposed features are competitive with expert-engineered descriptors, which makes the LLM most useful as a feature proposer working alongside deterministic chemistry tools.

Research projectOptimization in the low-data regime

  • Studied how transfer learning helps when labelled data is scarce: pretrained a two-layer neural network on MNIST digits, then fine-tuned only the final layer on related, unrelated and out-of-domain targets (FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10).
  • Benchmarked fine-tuned models against trained-from-scratch baselines at six dataset sizes from 10 to 1,000 samples per class, using AdamW, early stopping, and both test accuracy and cross-entropy loss.
  • Found that fine-tuning helps most at the smallest sizes (53.4% → 59.3% accuracy with 10 samples per class on related digits), that the gap narrows as data grows, and that it brings little benefit under strong domain mismatch.
